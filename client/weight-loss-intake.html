Questions, answered

What to know before you begin.

Final clinical, pharmacy, pricing, and state-availability language should be reviewed before launch.

Who may be eligible for GLP-1 treatment?

Eligibility depends on factors such as health history, current medications, weight-related goals, and the clinician’s judgment. Completing an assessment does not guarantee a prescription.

How do GLP-1 medications support weight loss?

GLP-1 medicines act on pathways involved in appetite, fullness, and blood-sugar regulation. The physician will explain whether a particular option may be appropriate for you.

What side effects should I know about?

Side effects vary by medication and person. Gastrointestinal symptoms can occur. Your clinician will review risks, warning signs, and what to do if you have a concern before treatment begins.

How much does medication cost?

Medication costs are separate. If treatment is clinically appropriate, Your doctor will discuss the available options and their costs with you before a prescription is filled.

What support tools are available?

MMD may offer fitness and nutrition tools such as guided exercise, meal planning, and progress tracking to support practical routines. Availability and final app details will be confirmed with your care team.

Do I need a video appointment?

No. Video is optional and is not required to submit the intake. You can begin with free ongoing asynchronous doctor follow-up by secure text; your doctor typically responds within a few hours. A physician may still determine that an evaluation by video is clinically needed. If you choose an optional Zoom video visit, it costs $39 per visit one time; purchase the upgrade after the free intake, then arrange the appointment after payment.

Is ongoing text-based care free?

Yes. Ongoing asynchronous doctor follow-up by secure text is free, not just the first conversation. Medication costs are separate, and a prescription is not guaranteed. Optional Zoom video visits with a doctor specializing in medical weight loss cost $39 per visit one time; purchase after the free intake and arrange the appointment after payment.

Are compounded medications FDA-approved?

No. Compounded medications are not FDA-approved. If a compounded option is considered, your clinician should explain why and discuss relevant risks and alternatives.
